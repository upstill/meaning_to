Against the Canonical Ideal

Unalloyed striving is almost always a bad idea

We human beings want matters to be clear. A big part of organizing yourself for life is deciding what you believe and what matters the most to you. This is such an important job that we hardly notice when we take it one step further, into a hunt for general principles that we can depend on to be true; goals we can pursue relentlessly; ideals we can use to measure ourselves solely by how much we serve them. The opposite of tradeoffs and paradoxes.

Does that seem like a meaningless distinction? It turns out to be anything but. 

We all know people who take it too far.

Most of us even have an essential skill: pretending that a principle is absolute while realizing that exceptions are hard to avoid. (murder)

Traumatizing children? How about chemo? Saving them from abuse by removing them from their parents?


  • not taking up too much space
  • not offending anyone or bruising their feelings. (rupture and repair)
