Fighting Climate Change is Not That Hard

Conversation about climate change is rife with alarums and excursions. Typical of our modern political dialogue, the conversation is at a fever pitch. The urgency to take action is matched by an equally strident resistance, by concerns that the scenarios are uncertain and the cost of mitigation not worth it. With both sides banging heads, progress is uncertain at best and half-assed at worst.

I would like to suggest that most of this sturm und drang is wasted. The kind of real change that’s needed is not going to happen until everybody just calms. The Hell. Down. 

The dynamics at hand

My thesis here is simple: that both climate alarmists and skeptics are engaged in a tacit conspiracy to make action to mitigate climate catastrophe seem as hard as possible. 

Let’s put on our Manichean glasses and divide the contenders into the Crusaders and the Resisters. The Crusaders honestly perceive a world under threat; consequently, they are strident about it, and prepared to pay a concomitantly high price to head off that threat, including collapsing whole sectors of the economy, building new ones, disrupting careers by the millions, spending trillions of dollars along the way. The Resisters are less certain of the threat and alarmed about the cost of addressing it, and they get their traction from the costs and uncertainty involved.

So both sides are committed to a vision of great cost and effort, one side saying “Hell yeah!”, the other saying “Not worth it!” But as the issue rages on, the worse this dynamic gets. The more desperate and strident the Crusaders get, the more costly appears. And the Resisters have their own interest in making the costs seem excessive, the better to Resist undertaking them. Both sides have an interest in bidding up the cost of action.
