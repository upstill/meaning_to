The Price of Rage

A long time ago, I was having dinner with Michael, a reasonably well-placed executive at a large publishing house, who was clearly downtrodden and dispirited by the corporate infighting that was engulfing him. In his despair, he introduced me to a concept that’s stayed with me ever since. He described his colleagues as:

People who would rather have their organization as a whole fail than see their rivals within the organization succeed.

For lack of a better term, I’ll call this the Burn It Down principle.

You know who these people are, and how they are: so blinded by the needs of their own ego that they can’t see any picture beyond dominating or undermining their rivals:

  • the spouse who would prefer their relationship to crash and burn rather than admit they were wrong;
  • people who wield a “nuclear option” to assert control over others who are more shy to go there;
  • countries at war, each of whom would sooner see their economies collapse than surrender.

To be clear: social life often involves competition, zero-sum games in which the parties involved are wrangling for spoils. Some win, and some lose. These competitions may be lively, and they can even be vicious. But Burning It Down is about something else, a selfishness that goes beyond wanting to deprive others for your own benefit, to depriving others with no benefit to yourself — or even serious cost— for the sole purpose of beating them into submission…or extinction. It’s even worse if the organization includes innocent stakeholders who also go down with the ship.

The Burn It Down principle draws us a line, separating the vigorously competitive from the truly malicious. Cross that line and you enter a whole different realm of human engagement. I’ve heard Stephen King (who should know) define evil as the conscious will to do harm. How far off is this?

Think about America today and I bet you can tell where this is going.

The US has a long history of intemperate politics, especially in its first century. Exaggeration, lies, demonization of rivals, confirmation bias, cherry-picked data, echo chambers and all the rest of it are just part of the territory — across the political spectrum. There’s plenty of blame to go around for a breakdown of civility. It takes two to pump a vicious cycle.

For my money, all of that comes under the heading of lively competition, no matter how heated it gets. You could take the temperature of the current political climate and say that it’s pretty hot out there, but also, “same as it ever was”. Still, there’s something new in the air, something special about our times. There’s a whole political class who are willing to Burn It Down, and Donald Trump is both their inspiration and exemplar par excellence.

Consider how face coverings against COVID have become a political football. One could interpret requirements to wear a mask as a simple public health measure along the lines of not shitting in the streets. And one could think about how trifling the inconvenience of a mask is, compared to the the economic cataclysm of lockdowns and business closures to fight it. But apparently, for large swaths of the population, wearing a mask just means giving in to the opposition. They are unwilling to lift a finger, figuratively speaking, to join the fight and help inhibit the pandemic, just because it would mean conceding to “the elites”.

I’m no historian, so I trace the beginning of this era back to the early days of the Obama presidency, when Mitch McConnell said outright that it would be the policy of the Republican Party to resist every initiative of the incoming administration, lest Obama get credit for anything good coming out of his governance: “The single most important thing we want to achieve is for President Obama to be a one-term president.” Paraphrasing: if it’s good for the country, it’s good for Obama’s re-election prospects. The House Speaker at the time, John Boehner, had a similar sentiment about Obama’s agenda: “We’re going to do everything — and I mean everything we can do — to kill it, stop it, slow it down, whatever we can.” Doesn’t matter if it’s a good idea or not; doesn’t matter if it’s good for the country or not; if it’s got Obama’s name on it, it’s bad for us.

We’ve travelled a long road since then — almost twelve long years. Now we have a President who is, not to put too fine a point on it, a stain on democracy; who has made the US the pity of the world; and who enjoys the unyielding support of about one in three Americans. When you talk to those supporters, do they care about the lying, the haplessness in the face of COVID, the diminished position of America in the world, the trashing of health and climate change policy, the elevation of narcissism to a governing principle? No doubt there are Trump supporters who truly like him for his policies, but you don’t have to dig too deeply to find a whole swath whose attachment to the man revolves around his impulsive diarrhea-of-the-Tweet, his delight in “sticking it” to the libs, the apoplexy he triggers in his/their opponents. Those T-shirts say it all: “I’d rather be Russian than a Democrat.”

I say that Trump are these people’s prime exemplar because in the tradeoff between what is good for him and what is good for the USA, the latter doesn’t even figure. Does anyone doubt that he would ride this country to destruction before admitting defeat? Even his Press Secretary, whose mission is to make him look good, was silenced by being asked what it would take for Trump to accept the results of the 2020 election.

The history of Donald Trump on the national stage is the history of a man who truly does put his own interests over those of the country he “serves” — shamelessly, because he can’t even imagine why anyone would have a problem with that.

  • He has backhanded concerns about foreign electoral interference in the age of social media. From the moment it appeared that he had the benefit of Russian bots he has taken the idea as a personal affront, setting the US up as an open target for Russian and Chinese brain-hacking.
  • He has put his gut feelings and his political agenda over the findings of the professional intelligence community, to the point of pressuring the intelligence agencies to tailor their reports to his needs.
  • He has turned the Department of Justice into his personal political tool.
  • He has thrown around the “fake news” epithet, and made it a point of pride to get his supporters to swallow a daily or even hourly stream of lies and bullshit (his inauguration crowds, Birtherism, the Central Park Five, the “success” of his leadership on the pandemic), to the point that the idea of truth itself — information that is essential for meaningful dialogue to even begin — has crumbled as a standard, replaced with informational nihilism.
  • He has trashed long-standing norms of Presidential behavior, from releasing tax returns, to putting his businesses in a blind trust, to emoluments, to using the White House as a political stage for the RNC.
  • He has personally roamed consequence-free through the fields of behavior ranging from the dubious (nepotism, using AG Barr as his personal tool), to the unprecedented (undermining the Mueller investigation, stonewalling Congress, flouting the will of the Supreme Court) to the criminal (the Stormy Daniels payoff, shaking down the president of Ukraine, violating the Hatch Act with his acceptance speech) — all of it because the integrity of the system itself counts for nothing when weighed against his personal political needs.

These actions are a special kind of awful, deeply corrosive to foundations of civic life that we hardly appreciated before we saw them under siege — foundations that have grown and solidified since the Founding, some planned, some not, but all of them definitional to How America Works: the ability to start discussions with agreed-upon facts; an independent Justice Department; an intelligence community doing its work independent of politics; a fair election system; the protection of whistleblowers and proper management of their claims; the watchdog role of a free press; the investigatory power of Congress; honoring judicial rulings; a presidency scrubbed clean of even the appearance of self-interest, with transparency, divestment and putting assets into blind trusts.

Misguided policies can (mostly) be reversed by a change of administration. But norms and standards get their force from precedent, so this damage may be irreversible. Standards violated are standards degraded, now and in the future. As dubious as Nixon’s actions in Watergate were, at least he had the decency to comply with court orders and deliver the tapes to Congress. Now, it’s more important for Donald Trump to win, than it is to preserve the integrity of the system that sustains us, that makes American government what it is. He’ll Burn It Down before he’ll lose.

And as the 2020 election looms, we get the most telling example yet: the controversy over how the election will be conducted, and whether the results will be valid.

Let’s not be naive: the electoral system in the US is hardly a blushing virgin. Like heated rhetoric, hanky-panky at the ballot box has always been with us. We’ve had whispers about the election of JFK and LBJ’s ascension to the Senate, to say nothing of Jim Crow-era suppression of the Black vote.

Nonetheless. We generally don’t talk much about that in public, because we venerate elections: they more or less are democracy. Respecting the sanctity of the vote — even knowing of its occasional lapses — is a powerful norm, so powerful that you can point to politicians who acted to sustain that norm even to their own detriment. After the 1960 election of JFK, there were enough rumors (since discredited) about suspect voting in Richard Daley’s Chicago that Richard Nixon could have incited a national crisis by calling it out — but even Nixon was patriotic enough to stand down and go quietly into defeat. And Al Gore could have contested the 2000 election for months — but he didn’t, because the cost to the country was so much greater than the benefit to himself.

And now there’s Donald Trump, and those for whom he speaks. The President is not just disputing the results of an election after the fact, when there might be evidence to be evaluated and specific recourse to be pursued. He is calling the results into question beforehand, based on nothing but speculation and an assumption of malfeasance — felonious, seditious malfeasance — by Democrats. This is a move of the deepest cynicism, attacking one more key institution solely for his own political benefit.

Wait, what’s that I hear from our friends on the Right? Mail-in voting is a recipe for massive fraud? I won’t argue that point, because it doesn’t matter. This “controversy” is not about the integrity of the system. It is purely about softening the ground for Donald Trump to undermine the results if the election doesn’t go his way, and he doesn’t care a whit about the system. This is not just an opinion, it’s a demonstrable fact. May I demonstrate?

Let’s assume the opposite, that the President and his allies actually do care about the proper execution of democracy; really are sincerely concerned that the 2020 election be conducted impeccably, such that its results will stand without question. What would you see if that were true? A President who had that concern at top of mind would be doing everything in his power, beginning with the use of the bully pulpit, to make that happen. Here are a few things such a President might do:

  • Make an inspiring and uplifting speech about the importance of a reliably functioning election system, such that you know the President understands its importance, and the urgency of making it work right under the challenge of the pandemic.
  • Assemble a task force to evaluate the security and reliability of systems used by various states for mail-in ballots, including a printing process that resists counterfeiting (the Mint can probably help with that), and auditing the process of signature verification.
  • Make it clear that tampering with mail-in ballots or interfering with their delivery is a federal crime that will be vigorously prosecuted, and with great fanfare create an Election Security Unit at the FBI.
  • Make the process to vote in person a matter of civil rights by monitoring polling places and penalizing governments where some citizens have to queue up for hours to exercise their franchise.
  • Ensure that the Postal Service is in tip-top shape for postmarking and delivering ballots in a timely manner.
  • Take responsibility for protecting the vote with a relentless drumbeat of messages from the White House reassuring voters that this election will take place in an open, secure fashion and its results will stand, both numerically and morally. Because that’s who our country is.
  • Turn every stone and take every step to ensure that the election is pristine of any foreign interference, or even the suspicion thereof.

Notice that all of these possibilities are completely non-partisan and, I would argue, deeply patriotic. But as far as Donald Trump is concerned, they are strictly from Fantasyland. Will you hear any such messages, get any such actions from Donald Trump or his supporters? It is to laugh. And that’s how we know that they would rather burn down the system than let their opponents prevail. This noise about a corrupt election — built on air and speculation — is 100% about burning another institution up to fuel political advantage, and 0% about defending that institution in the interests of the country it serves.

To be clear, I don’t resent Trump partisans their anger. I don’t hold their enemies blameless, in the sense that there is room for grievance in the heartland, whether the intent was malicious or not. They’re not imagining that globalism has hollowed out the American working class, to the benefit of the coastal management class. They’re not imagining that the Left has energetically resorted to judicial activism when they couldn’t advance their agenda via legislation. They’re not imagining a news media biased against them. One can argue that “the truth has a well-known liberal bias”, and it’s true that the more conservative sections of the populace often lag behind, for example, science, and social/economic understanding. (Need I say more than “evolution”, or “climate change”?) To those who are left behind this looks and feels like railroading. Once the perception took root that the coastal elites were taking unfair advantage of their power, position and isolation from “the rest of us”, the die was cast. Remember, Fox News was started explicitly to counter this bias no matter what the cost or how much it further degraded the dialogue.

But this is exactly my point: people on the Right are entitled to their anger in their impatience to be heard and heeded. Anger is, in fact, what gets the leadership’s attention. My point is that there’s a line between intemperate dialogue (and God knows the US has a rich history of that) and wishing your enemies so much ill that you’re willing to pay for your vengeance with national chaos, to bring the country down to get at them. And this is the line that the Right of today is exuberantly trouncing. This is what Trump gleefully foments in his supporters. On one side is lively (to put it mildly) debate and disputation. On the other side is nihilism.

Whether this situation developed from malicious intent or from the arguably correct policies of the people in power, the outcome is the same: the fabric of democracy in the USA — the norms and institutions that are the unsung bulwarks against chaos — is being destroyed willfully, even gleefully, by the words, deeds and ballot markers of Donald Trump and his people. One can blame them, and one can lay the blame at the feet of a new-media industry of paranoia, outrage and conspiracy theories. But regardless of the cause, this is NOT something you can say about the Democratic resistance.

I don’t imagine that any Trump supporter will ever read this far, but I wish I could have a conversation with them, and ask them something: Do you support Donald Trump because you’re really convinced that he and his policies (giving up on climate change, letting infrastructure rot, attacking Obamacare, bungling COVID management) are good for the country? Or do you support him because he’s not one of those people you hate, and he has it in for them just as much as you do? If you had to make a choice, which would it be? The ruination of people you’re fed up with, or a stronger, more admirable country?

In other words, are you willing to Burn It Down to get your point across?

I fear this is where we are. Modern politics has degraded in a way that is not a matter of degree, but of kind. When one side truly is ready to preside over the destruction of the very foundations of the country to get their way (or just to keep their opponents from getting their way), then we truly do have a crisis on our hands.
