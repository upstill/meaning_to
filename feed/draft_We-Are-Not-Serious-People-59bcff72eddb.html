We Are Not Serious People

How fucked is America? It’s a question that haunts the days of Donald Trump’s presidency. Sooner or later he’ll be gone, but how much of the damage is recoverable? It’s always fun to speculate (How long to re-earn trust from the international community? To restore institutional norms? To rein in future power grabs and corruption?), but I believe there’s one core truth that’s not speculative and it’s damning. Give me a few paragraphs to set it up.

[Spoiler Alert for the TV series Succession]

Succession is about the struggles of Logan Roy’s four children to become the successor to his vast media empire. After three seasons of teases and power plays in which one or another of them seem destined to win and/or struggle with the old man for control, the old man dismisses them all, delivering one of the most devastating put-downs ever to his privileged, delusional, narcissistic brood, making it clear that none of them is The One: “You’re not serious people.” He would rather cash out than entrust his empire to any of his offspring. It’s a killer stroke in the manner of many great dramatic reveals: it exposes, in the simplest possible sentence, a crucial truth that had been just below the surface all along.

My point is that there’s a similar, similarly cutting, observation about where we are just now: 

The United States of America is no longer a serious country in the charge of serious people. And that’s why we’re fucked.

That’s why we will keep thrashing around like a motorboat with no rudder. That’s why we’ll keep spending our energies attacking each other rather than tackling real issues. That’s why we’ll still be vulnerable to the siren song of authoritarianism. We’re no longer a nation of serious people.

How can we tell? How can we prove a negative like this? You can’t, of course: negatives are funny that way. Instead, I’m going to list out some qualities of serious people, and I think the differences between them and us will speak for themselves.

Serious people use reality to form their beliefs, not the other way round.

Serious people care about character and strive to embody it themselves.

Serious people factor in more than their own self-interest

There was a time when Americans took into account their national interest as well as their own. Think about the national response in World War II: widespread recuitment, dealing with privation rather than complaining about it, etc. 

Look at Canada in 2026, which has gone from an unserious nation in the US mold to a seriously serious one almost overnight:

[B]efore Trump started threatening to swallow Canada whole, Canadian politics was drifting toward the same lazy, vibes-based, punish-the-incumbent sleepwalk that infects every comfortable democracy on the planet. Then the human tariff schedule with a comb-over started ranting about annexation, and 40 million Canadians collectively sat bolt upright in bed and said righto, fuck this for a joke, no more mucking about, we are electing the most qualified adult on the continent.

Serious people heed wisdom over impulse

Serious people respect their own limitations 

Serious people know the difference between the serious and the frivolous, the real and the symbolic

Serious people accept the responsibility that comes with power.

Serious people prioritize the success of the enterprise over beating their rivals

How would we know that we were serious people? 

Serious people would take the national debt seriously.

Serious people would take the problem of housing supply head on.

Serious people would figure out a way to move forward on health care

Serious people would come to terms with immigration policy

Serious people take character seriously, even against their own self-interest

If you’d like to see a serious person in action, consider the Prime Minister of Canada. He is a member in good standing of the reality-based community. He will not cave to the whims of Donald Trump. He is a man of sober thought and thoughtful action
