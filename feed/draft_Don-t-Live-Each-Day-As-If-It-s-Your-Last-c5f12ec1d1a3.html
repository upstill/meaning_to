Don’t Live Each Day As If It’s Your Last

There’s a better way to get your head screwed on straight.

There are a lot of silly ideas floating around out there, and one of the silliest is the injunction to “live each day as if it’s your last.” I can’t be alone in imagining what it would actually be like to do that. If I had only a day to live, I promise I would be thrashing around in an unholy stew of feelings, mostly panic, both about the impending deadline and how to wind up my earthly affairs. Serene appreciation of my brief remaining time on earth? Not me.

Still, the thought behind the idea is a good one. We get so bogged down in the day-to-day that it’s hard to appreciate the life we have — and I mean really hard. The plain fact is that everyone likely to read this is living beyond the wildest dreams of the kings and cardinals of the past, and of most people living today. Simply having access to antibiotics, surgical anesthetic, dental care, adequate nutrition, air travel, personal automobiles, safe maternity, remarkable personal security, the supercomputer in our pockets — the list goes on — would be a laughable fantasy for 99% of the people who have ever lived — to say nothing of the ongoing wonder of nature, science, art and human relationships.

We should be hyperventilating with pleasure and astonishment at our lot. What’s wrong with us that we aren’t?
