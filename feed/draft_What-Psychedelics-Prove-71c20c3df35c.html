What Psychedelics Prove

Have you ever endured hearing about other people’s magical, transformative experiences with psychedelics? Whether you’ve had such experiences yourself or not, maybe you’ve learned to dread crackpot rants about alternative dimensions, mystical creatures and/or a drill down into the ultimate ground of being. What could be more tedious when received back here in quotidian reality?

As one who has had such experiences myself, I’m 

Under LSD, I have seen reality as we know it drop out altogether, “revealing” the cosmos as nothing but a flow of energy, with the people around me become pulsations of that energy.

Under MDMA, I have envisioned myself at the bottom of a swimming pool, swathed in tranquility and contentment, looking up at the surface with all the people churning and thrashing in their confusion, and thinking to myself “This is what it’s all about. This is what everyone’s trying to get to, whether they know it or not.”

But there’s always the voice in the back of my head that won’t shut up, saying “Yeah, well, that’s just the drugs talking”, or “What is there, really?” These experiences come laden with doubts that haunt serious people: mightn’t it all be a hallucination, a Coleridge revery with no intrinsic, material meaning?

Is any of that “real”? Is there any reason to think that these experiences have any more substance than a temporary shift in brain chemistry? Subjectively, absolutely. The sense of certainty that accompanies these flights is so complete as to be unquestionable — from the inside. From the outside, or even from the inside following the return to earth, can’t they all be filed away with dreams and delusions?

The “undeniable fact” in the title here is this:

Whatever the empirical validity of any psychedelic experience, one thing has to be true: the fact that my consciousness is capable of entering that state.

Just for one brief example: I have taken a group journey (MDMA) with seven other people. In the course of it, we did bonding exercises that, unsurprisingly, worked a treat. The group came out of the experience with a love that is palpable in their ordinary interactions today. 

That experience raises the question: is that love illusory? I’m here to tell you that it’s very real today. Is that current fact in any way diminished by its origin? 

Do you know what a catalyst is? In chemistry, a catalyst is a material that engenders a reaction among other substances, but the catalyst itself isn’t consumed. In the old tale of Stone Soup, the stone is unchanged at the end, but the village has been fed.

There’s an old joke that I keep coming back to again and again. It ends Woody Allen’s film Annie Hall (sorry), and even there it’s presented as an old joke:

[T]his guy goes to a psychiatrist and says, “Doc, my brother’s crazy. He 
 thinks he’s a chicken.” And the doctor says, “Well, why don’t you turn 
 him in?” And the guy says, “I would, but I need the eggs.”
