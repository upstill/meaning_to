The Phenomenology Crisis: Just how stupid are we, anyway?

We’re stewing in the evidence, but we just won’t accept the simple fact: people are stupid and getting stupider with every passing year.

I don’t mean “stupid” as in “blundering around bumping heads and breaking necks against reality”; Idiocracy stupid, Darwin Awards stupid. Our lives are generally arranged so that we can mostly avoid most fatal errors even with limited brainpower. I mean “stupid” as in “manifestly incapable of keeping up with the demands of modernity.”

The digital age has put humanity in a fatal bind: we are expected to know more and more at the same time that social media and the politicization of news have made it harder and harder to think coherently. If we keep going this way, democracy will fail and the only successful cultures will be ones where strong figures take over policy power and wrest control from contentious factions

A responsible thinker needs coherent beliefs far in excess of the human capacity for processing information. We have no choice but to use shortcuts/heuristics. There’s no getting around this fundamental limitation, regardless of education, dedication to truth.

We think the limitations on developing true beliefs have to do with intelligence, education and good faith. This implies that “some people” can dependably find their way to true beliefs, and enables the hope that we ourselves could be one of the epistemically virtuous. What we never stop to realize is that the real limiting factor is time, and none of us has much more time than anyone else. Some may use their time better than others, but no matter how well we use our time, it will never be enough. The time we have is no match for the time we need. Ultimately we’re all in the same boat, flailing against this limitation. We’re doomed to failure, and the only real difference between us is how dismally we fail.
