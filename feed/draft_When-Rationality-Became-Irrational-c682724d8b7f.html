When Rationality Became Irrational

So much of what’s right about modern life owes its presence in the world to Science. The legacy of rigorous, skeptical, empirical inquiry bequeathed to us by the Enlightenment has rendered a world unrecognizable compared to what came before — for good and ill. The good news is overwhelmingly good, but so is the bad, and we tend to take that as a take-it-or-leave-it proposition. The thing is, the two can be teased apart. It is possible to identify where Rationalism went wrong, and that would go a long way to getting humanity back on track.

The Enlightenment’s Original Sin

In the early 1700’s, an earthquake shook the world. If there is a turn of events that shifted the history of the world more quickly or comprehensively than the Enlightenment, I’d like to hear about it.

The great discovery of the Enlightenment, the truly earth-shattering revelation, was that the Universe didn’t have to be an inscrutable mystery that could only be (partially) explained by resorting to gods and prophets. People could, themselves, find truth in a process of proposal and experimentation.

At the heart of Enlightenment values is skeptical enquiry, and at the heart of skeptical enquiry is this stunningly brilliant move: let’s give up the quest for final, unquestionable Truth, for immovable ground on which to stand. Instead, let’s leave every proposition, no matter how solid it seems, up to questioning and revision or replacement.

This model of skeptical enquiry is the finest engine ever devised for getting closer to The Truth, exactly because it gives up on the idea of ever getting there.

The Enlightenment model of skeptical enquiry is truly one of the most cunning schemes ever invented. It said that the hunt for solid epistemological ground to stand on begins by giving up the idea of ever having permanent such ground.

And yet, today, ironically enough, there is a tidal wave of skepticism about the fruits of the Enlightenment, and even its core values of “Reason, Science, Humanism, and Progress”, as enumerated in the title of Steven Pinker’s defense of same, Enlightenment Now.

Medicine: organism vs. whole human

There is a case to be made (and Pinker makes it, vociferously) that rational enquiry is itself revealing the blind spots of the rational worldview, i.e. the cure for irrational rationality is more rationality. For example, medicine just is revealing the importance of bedside manner and placebos. Modern physics itself identified the shortcomings of Newton’s mechanistic universe. 

But it is also true that rationality enquiry is uncovering the limitations of rationality itself. Godel’s Incompleteness Theorem says it outright: no system of logic that is internally consistent can include all true propositions. In other words, no matter what logical system you choose, if it is consistent then there will be truths that lie outside it.
