One Crisis to Rule Them All

Civic life in the USA is at a boiling point. Incredibly, it has reached the point that the country may go to crisis about the legitimacy of the upcoming election. Does anyone think that civil war is out of the question? That question would have been unaskable four years ago.

There’s an understandable reflex to lay the blame at the feet of Donald Trump, that relentless agent of chaos. If Trump is the problem, it’s equally natural to look to the election for our salvation — and it certainly matters who takes office in 2021. Nonetheless, it’s time to realize that even a change of administration won’t save us. Much as I would love to blame Trump for everything wrong with America, it’s not true. Donald Trump would still be just a failing real estate developer in New York but for a host of circumstances that were swelling up when he came along to ride the wave. Donald Trump is as much an expression of what ails us as its cause.

So what does ail us? The circumstances that beget Trump are both multifarious and nefarious, but I am here to suggest a cause that lurks beneath them all, one crisis behind so many others. To get there, I need to start with an astonishing reality that we hardly even notice: the gobsmacking prevalence of truly preposterous beliefs in the public sphere.

A Carnival of Craziness

If the Internet and global communications were supposed to do anything, they were to democratize information, sweeping away propaganda, rumor and disinformation in a flood of Truth. Indeed, the supercomputer in our pockets offers us that option today.

But that’s not all. The Internet has also weaponized nonsense. There are thriving, if not viral, communities of people who sincerely believe things like:

  • The earth is flat and the moon landings were fake.
  • 9–11 was a putup job coordinated and executed by a cabal of thousands who operated without a single one ever defecting or leaving a trace, planting explosive charges in the Twin Towers and managing a dual airstrike to conceal the actual operation.
  • The leadership of the Democratic party is a pack of satanic, cannibalistic pedophiles who will be vanquished by Donald Trump working with Robert Mueller.
  • COVID-19 is a fabrication of the ruling elites to dominate the hoi polloi. Or a biological strike at the US by China. Or something.
  • Anthropogenic global warming is a hoax perpetrated by a conspiracy of scientists to hobble human progress.
  • The American healthcare system is the envy of the world.
  • Joe Biden is the new Red Menace whose guiding purpose is to destroy the suburbs, hand the economy back to China and transform the US into a Socialist autocracy.

Obviously, conspiracy theories and misinformation are nothing new. But the scope and scale of this infection would have been the stuff of dystopian fiction just a few years ago. The election under siege. Families riven by political division. Respectful dialogue a wistful memory. How can a country act on anything if it can’t even face in the same direction? And we’re seeing the answer: it can’t.

This United States of America of 2020 is literally going insane. And it’s not all Donald Trump’s fault.

How does this happen? There must be something seriously wrong for so many millions of brains to be infected like this — something new in the air.

The Meltdown

Think of public discourse like a nuclear chain reaction. In a reactor, uranium atoms decay, sending off neutrons that can kick more atoms into decay; if the chain reaction isn’t controlled somehow it enters a vicious cycle that ends in a meltdown — or an atomic explosion.

What controls a nuclear reactor is the presence of carbon rods mingled with the uranium. The rods absorb some fraction of the free neutrons, dampening the chain reaction and keeping it simmering along at a steady, productive, energy-producing pace rather than melting down or dying out.

In a sane society, ideas engage and spark off each other, producing both heat and light. But the most insidious memes are like the unconstrained neutrons of pure uranium: people and cohorts in close proximity can literally drive each other insane, with half-baked ideas, overheated rhetoric and deliberate misinformation that spark off more of the same. Without some inhibitory factor, a vicious cycle emerges, in which people believe crazier and crazier things, more and more vehemently.

In this analogy, the Internet has done two things: gathered uranium into tighter and tighter pockets, so that free neutrons have more reactive effect; and removed the buffering effect of information provided by people in the traditional media whose job is to put a damper on nonsense. Dismissing “the mainstream media” and encouraging everyone to live in their own reality just pulls out those carbon rods.

The “one crisis to rule them all” is a looming meltdown in how we gather information and intercommunicate, to the point that we no longer live in the same reality. The outcome of the 2020 election will do nothing to change this. As I said, civil war is no longer out of the question. This is why.

But what is special about this particular moment? There have always been information silos on the Internet, and Fox News started in the last century. Why is this meltdown happening now, of all times?

I do have a devil in mind, and this devil has a name, albeit a disarmingly boring one: The Attention Economy.

If you don’t pay for the product, you are the product

Think of the astronomical value of Facebook, Google/YouTube and Twitter. Did you contribute a single cent to any of them? Not if you’re a typical user you didn’t. And yet many of the founders of these companies are among the wealthiest people in history. Where did those vast fortunes come from?

Everyone knows the superficial answer: showing you ads. However, the important thing is not what rings the cash register, but what it takes to get to that point.

Consider another obvious fact: Facebook can’t show you an ad if you’re on YouTube. Google can’t deploy AdSense at you if you’re using DuckDuckGo — and none of them can make money if you’re out hanging with your friends or playing with your kids. For all of these companies, cashflow ceases if they don’t have your attention. For any “free” service, the game is played by securing the eyeballs of people by the millions. Everything revolves around showing them what gets, and keeps, their attention.

It’s hard to grasp the scope and scale of the technology devoted to this one goal; thinking about the money being made may help to imagine it. It’s no exaggeration to say that on the other side of your phone’s screen you have a supercomputer pointed at your brain armed with information about your past behavior and devoted to keeping your attention. On the other hand, our brains offer a rich set of handles to exploit: morbid curiosity; fear; tribal identity; righteous wrath; confirmation of prior beliefs: the whole toolbox of human manipulation.

Think of the movements full of people whose attention is a sure thing: QAnon; Flat Earthers; 911 truthers; climate deniers. You, dear reader, can sit in your armchair and describe how to enlist new recruits for any of these if you know a person’s prior inclinations. In fact, here’s a thought experiment: imagine you set out to play the world’s worst practical joke by recruiting someone you know well into a conspiracy theory or an extremist organization. You have continuous, complete control over what their phone suggests for them, whether on their Facebook timeline, their Twitter or News feeds, or their YouTube recommendations. You can experiment endlessly in a search for what actually grabs them and gets a click. Once interest is aroused, you can present them with content that entices them further: gives them a little bit more information, nudges them further down the rabbit hole. Baby step by baby step, you bring them into the company of like-minded others who bring them further into the fold. Before long, you’ve set up a vicious circle in which their identity becomes fused with their new fellows and reinforced by new information/spin/encouragement/discussion. And your new recruit never had to be aware of any of this. All they had to do was click on what seemed interesting.

But of course, this process isn’t being driven by amateur armchair recruiters. It’s being driven by algorithms of astronomical scale and precision, armed with all the information people have revealed about themselves online and controlling what gets suggested to them. What else do you need to know about the formation of QAnon, or the Flat Earth movement?

Naked in Public

Consider Google’s contributions to your life: Android, YouTube, gmail, Photos, Docs, Drive, Calendar, Contacts, Maps, not to mention Search. Every one is an absolute fount of information about you. To what purpose is that information put? Getting and holding your attention by using that information to exploit basic human drives.

In a way, Facebook is your most intimate friend: they are privy to all the information (the thoughts, the opinions, the recommendations, the incidents of your life) you’ve shared with all your other friends, as well as who those friends are and their information. If Facebook is going to show you ads, they need to keep your attention — by any means necessary.

To be clear: these companies did not set out to undermine Civilization As We Know It. The insanity around us is a distant, unintended effect of their fiscal need to sell ads by getting your attention. This whole nightmare is simply the outcome of AI systems figuring out how to maximize people’s attention. Even the AIs don’t have a theory of human mind. They just know what works.

It’s also worth noting that the outrage machine is not limited to social networks. You could sign off the Internet altogether and still get all the outrage you want from broadcast media. The founding charter of Fox News was to stir up the Right, and MSNBC serves a similar function for the Left. Indeed, the supporters of Donald Trump seem connected by a widespread, self-sustaining network that gets nourishment in a variety of ways.

I throw up my hands trying to convey the scale of what’s going on here, because 1) I actually can’t fit it into my own head; 2) you can’t succeed without sounding like a paranoid crank; and 3) these services really do go down so easy. You’re on Facebook for the personal connections. You’re on YouTube for the cat videos. You’re enmeshed with Google because Services! So many Services! All synchronized and working together!!! And yet…

What’s Really Going to Change?

So this is me giving up on properly communicating what’s actually going on. Instead, I can only remind you of the outcome: the absolutely ludicrous, delusional ideas that people have have come to embrace, and the meltdown of discourse as people get more tightly bound into hotter and hotter info-tribes. Something powerful made that happen. You don’t have to buy into, or even understand, the processes at work here. You only need to come to terms with the critical juncture we’re at, and make the connection back to what brought us here.

The election is still important. We shouldn’t imagine that there’s no difference between Donald Trump and Joe Biden. But in terms of where we are as a society, as a people who have to make decisions and move forward together, the bottom line is this:

As long as there are people and organizations whose livelihood depends on getting our attention by getting us angry at each other, America will continue heading for meltdown.

Now What?

Having highlighted a dire reality, I feel obliged to make suggestions for fixing it. Unfortunately, closed-form solutions are pretty thin on the ground. Even the outlines of the problem are just now coming into focus. So my suggestions are fairly vague, and centered around raising awareness.

First, come to terms with the existence of these systems. This essay is my contribution to the discussion, but it has been inspired by hearing Tristan Harris speak, and really fired up by The Social Dilemma, which is playing on Netflix. Absorbing it may not keep your next Trump-or-not-Trump “discussion” from going nuclear, but it will at least give you some understanding of why it does.

Second, realize that you are not immune. Get a grounding in the cognitive biases that make us all susceptible to conviction spirals and groupthink. Learn to recognize confirmation bias (filtering information for that which confirms your existing perspective) and the Dunning-Kruger Effect (in which the people who know the least about a subject think they’re an expert because they know so little), to name just two. I intend for my writing to focus on this sort of thing, but you could do worse than to dive into the work of David McRaney at You Are Not So Smart (podcast and website). Tim Urban at Wait But Why has an epic series of posts about how minds work together in a society, called The Story of Us.

Third, help make this kind of thing Not Okay. Society progresses when a phenomenon gets held up to the light of day and recognized as a Bad Thing. Child labor, overt racism and casual rape of the environment are just a few cases where society came to grips with an awful reality that had been invisible, taken for granted, “just the way things are”. And we can become more sophisticated about manipulation: just have a quick look back at the clumsy propaganda and laughably obvious advertising of only a few decades ago.

How does that work in everyday life? By talking about it. Noticing the symptoms when someone starts going down the rabbit hole, and calling it out. Pushing back when friends forward blatant bullshit that only works on their cohort. Awareness in public may not be enough, but it is definitely necessary.

There may be solutions in the political/regulatory realm, but there is no substitute for getting more sophisticated about what’s going on here: how it works, and what a serious pickle we’re in. America is barreling through a dark tunnel. We can all bring a little light.
