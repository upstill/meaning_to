Uncontrollable Rage For The Win!

When irrational anger is actually rational.

Uncontrollable Rage For the Win!

Irrational anger must be a bug in the software, right? The spouse who crashes their life partnership with an outburst. The driver who lashes out at a policeman and winds up in jail. The worker who tells his boss to take that job and shove it. When people fly off the handle in the heat of the moment, you have to wonder: why are we like that? What’s the advantage of building a species that can cut off its nose to spite its face? Wouldn’t this be a fatal bug for a social species?

Surprisingly, there is a perfectly rational reason for irrational anger, under certain circumstances. Can you guess how that works?

First, define “irrational anger” as an emotional reaction that costs the perpetrator more than they gain. Now, consider the case of the schoolyard bully. Every day, he shakes down his victim for their lunch money, and the victim hands it over because, well, what else are they to do? It’s not worth a beatdown to keep a few coins, is it? The rational thing is to just go along.

But suppose the bully picks the wrong victim. Suppose one victim is so outraged by the unfairness of the situation that they decide to do something about it. They’re so irrationally angry that they strike back, even knowing what it’s going to cost them.

Now consider it from the bully’s side. They can deliver the expected beating, but an enraged victim is going to do some amount of damage in return. Is it worth it? There’s a good chance that, faced with someone who doesn’t care that they’re getting worse than they give, the bully will retire from the field — or at least move on to another victim.

Chalk up a win for irrational anger. The victim’s willingness, thanks to their anger, to bear a cost greater than the immediate benefit to them, shifts the bully’s equation decisively.

Even the most primitive societies have an innate respect for the insane. — Motorcycle Boy, Rumblefish

There’s a certain advantage in being willing to pay more for power than it’s strictly worth — a payment that may never have to be made, once the willingness is established. But isn’t this why psychopaths so often prevail in struggles for power? They’re the only ones willing to take the losing risks and pay the excessive prices. The rest of us are too rational.

Ironic, isn’t it?
