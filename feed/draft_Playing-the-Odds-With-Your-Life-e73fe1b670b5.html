Playing the Odds With Your Life

We Americans, our minds are ruled by Horatio Alger.

It’s hard to grasp how important the Horatio Alger myth is to how Americans understand themselves. There’s something uniquely American about the idea that anyone can achieve anything with enough pluck and hard work. 

Traditional conservative politics were deeply rooted in that idea, and in the corollary that


What are the real odds of success — and does it matter?

  • making a living in movies/art
  • making a living in music
  • making a living writing books: even supposing you can climb the mountain of actually getting to the end of writing a readable book, getting an agent and finding a publisher, just 1% of books sell more than 5000 copies
  • having a successful startup
  • playing in the NFL/NBA/MLB
  • meeting the people that make it happen
  • playing the stock market
  • success in politics
  • funnels all over the place: where by definition only a few can succeed

The more you look, the more success looks like a lottery

The key paradox: anybody can become president, but not everybody

Takeaways:

  • Just because anybody can succeed doesn’t mean everybody can.
  • The odds are sometimes as bad as playing the lottery.
  • Luck plays an irreducible role in our success.
  • We should get out of the grip of Horatio Alger at least as far as appreciating that we can only take limited credit for success.
