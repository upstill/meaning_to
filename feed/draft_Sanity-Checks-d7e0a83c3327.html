Sanity Checks

One of the things that scares people off of mathematics, or any logical system, is the rigor. A logician really hasn’t done her job unless she’s dotted all the i’s and crossed all the t’s: you can’t build an airtight case if you leave anything out. And the idea of doing that, of building a complete, airtight case, is scary — especially as viewed from the outside because you’ve never done it. That’s why most folks are happy to cruise along on intuition and impression; logicians too, most of the time.

Despite my background in the rigors of computer science, my deep dark secret is that I share that aversion. So I really like the idea of a sanity check. Usually when we’re presented with a proposition or an idea, the goal is to decide definitively whether it’s true or false, which is a very high bar and not often worth the trouble of getting over it. A sanity check, on the other hand, doesn’t ask “Is it true?”, but rather “Is it sane? Does it make any kind of sense at all?” If it’s not sane, you can dismiss it out of hand. If it is sane, it may or may not be worth further consideration. But at least it’s not insane. A sanity check is a low-key way to get closer to the truth without going overboard.

An example:

“Believe all women” What if two women claim contradictory things?

“Character counts.” (Rush Limbaugh and others on Clinton, before getting behind Trump.)

“Fiscally conservative”

Does unfettered access to guns extend to POCs?

Was the War On Drugs ever about safety? See response to opioids.

Still awaiting test results

What if a bonafide study found that gay parenting couples had results somewhere between straight couples and absent-father households?

My lefty cohort is waging a pitched moral battle for voting rights, but it’s hard not to notice the political benefit, marginalized voters disproportionately coming down on the liberal side. But I wonder what would happen to that principle if it turned out that the voters thus graced weren’t quite the allies that one assumes.
