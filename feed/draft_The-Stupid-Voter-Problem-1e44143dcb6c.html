The Stupid Voter Problem

It’s sorely tempting for people like me to look at today’s electoral landscape and decide that a large fraction of Americans are just plain stupid. Personally, I’m grasping at straws trying to rationalize their behavior and avoid that conclusion, when I’m not doing handstands to avoid the question altogether. Ironically, that makes it harder to confront the question: what is wrong with the American voter?

 I know it’s hard to believe when we’re drowning in “information” and especially controversy, but there’s (at least) one question/topic that’s off-limits for serious discussion, addressed in only the most oblique way: what is wrong with the American voter?

I don’t mean that the word “stupid” isn’t slung around casually in online forums. I mean that the character and perspicacity of “the voter”, the ground-level participant in democracy, is an article of faith, and if you want to be taken seriously, then dropping any hint that some fraction of voters are letting the ball drop, is a good way to get yourself dismissed if not ostracized as elitist.

This crystallized for me recently when Jonathan V. Last of The Bulwark arguably stepped on that third rail in an episode of the Next Level podcast. In his frustration he made a few anodyne comments about that: 

Trust me, I understand why it’s such a hot topic. In America, any discussion that smacks of elitism — such as, oh, I don’t know, calling people stupid — is to be avoided at all cost, especially when the perception of elitism is such a hand grenade. Also, online “discussions” with or about one’s adversaries go first to anger and attacks on character. Finally, the spirit of “to everyone a vote” doesn’t play very well with the idea that some people thus enfranchised are idiots.
