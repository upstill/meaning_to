Dear 2021. This is 2050. We thought we should check in.

A Letter from the Future

Dear 2021,

This is your future calling — 2050 to be exact. We know it’s a bit disconcerting to hear from us like this, but we remember when you wrote to 1971. Do you? It seems like a long time ago, but it’s a good idea, every once in a while, to help our past selves buck up and face the future. 

Remember all the things you had to tell 1971? Institutions were in an uproar; young people were revolting (but aren’t they always, if you know what we mean…); women were marching in the streets to find their voice; America’s War On Drugs, The Counterculture And The Underclass was just gearing up; crime seemed like a never-ending plague; and on and on. It’s so important to have a little perspective, and we’re glad you were able to offer them yours. From 2021, violent crime as an overweening national concern is mainly a memory. Back in 1971, the odd woman who stuck out for doing anything outside home and hearth was really odd; but nowa(your)days, the prominence of women in leadership and achievement is, well, unremarkable. The word “smog” is enough to send young people to the dictionary. The Cold War is a distant memory. People of non-standard color and sexuality have come out from their cave into the daylight of your society. It’s dawning on your whole society that mind-altering substances are at least as viable an option as alcohol and potentially world-changing. The worst of world poverty and deprivation are vanishing at a miraculous pace. Even smoking cigarettes has gone from casual universality to weird quirk. (We notice you didn’t mention how quaint and naive their media and their political dialogue were, or let slip about the Internet or the supercomputers you have in your pockets. But we understand the sensitivities involved — hint hint.)

Most of those remarkable facts only arrived at the end of decades and tumult and tragedy, but they were all the stuff of fantasy for the people who received your letter. Which is why we’re writing this one to you now.

We can feel the pain throbbing in your era. But we want you to remember that the laurels of tomorrow are paid for with the struggles of today — even when those struggles seem to be driving you off a cliff.

Your year is just now coming to grips with a changing climate after decades of beating around the bush, and you are still looking at that challenge as a burdensome, costly effort to ward off disaster. It’s only now that we can see that the investment you put into rebuilding your world will actually pay for itself in terms of new economic activity and prosperity, especially lucrative jobs that must be done locally.

Your world, since 1971, has seen a bifurcation of wealth in which people living in tents and cashing in recyclables to eke out a daily meal live within sight of people who spend hundreds of millions of dollars on a painting just because they can. ’Twas ever thus, but ’tis moreso for you than ever. You cannot see the stress this puts on your social fabric, but we can tell you this: the tragedy of your situation is exactly what it takes to motivate a change, to define a level below which no one need fall, partly by trimming the excess that others have accrued beyond the needs of Croesus.

As with money, so with health care. Some of your countries have figured this one out, but America’s health “system” is so broken that this very brokenness birthed the will to fix it. By 2050, your people can concentrate on making themselves wealthy and wise rather than wondering if a medical emergency will bankrupt them — if they can get care at all.

Finally, and most importantly: you in America feel like you’re witnessing the final breakdown of civility, common purpose, even shared reality. Citizens by the millions trumpet beliefs about election integrity, the shape of the earth, vast conspiracies of child-eating politicians, tracking devices in vaccines — and more — that are strictly from Crazytown. Without giving away any spoilers, let’s just say that this kind of craziness is exactly what it takes to make change happen. Sitting here in 2050, we can recall the horrors of a population plunging into such madness, and for exactly that reason we are determined that it not happen here. We look at one another and nod knowingly, with a universal commitment to avoid these frailties that humans are prey to. We are hyper-vigilant for memes that tell us what we want to hear, or make us angry at the Other. We look warily at the Forward button, as though it has a little electric shock for us if we use it promiscuously. 

We do this not just to avoid re-enacting those days, but because we don’t want our fellows to take us for a buffoon. And we do it, frankly, because we’re better than you. But we only got that way because you were so lost. We know you can’t see that from where you are, but that’s pretty much what it means to be lost.

So we know that this letter will not have a material effect on you, any more than your letter did on 1971. But we do have one word of caution: the future from which you’re receiving this is only one future that the Multiverse has to offer. Finding your way to where we are is up to you. Choose wisely.

Regards,

2050
